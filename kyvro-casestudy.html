A fashion store for Bangladesh where anyone can open their own shop.

Kyvro sells clothes, shoes and accessories in BDT, with bKash, Nagad and cash on delivery. On top of the main store, any customer can start a reseller shop on their own link, set their own markup and get paid out, without holding stock or seeing the supplier.

// The context

Online fashion in Bangladesh runs on Facebook pages and trust

Most clothing is sold through Facebook pages and WhatsApp chats. Buyers expect cash on delivery, pay with bKash or Nagad rather than cards, and fake orders cost sellers money in wasted delivery. At the same time, many young people want to sell online but have no money for stock.

Kyvro answers both sides. It is a proper store with a real checkout, and a platform that lets people resell its products under their own shop name. Products come from local wholesale suppliers and are shipped by them, so neither Kyvro nor its sellers hold inventory.

// Hard problems

The decisions that mattered

01

Four roles, and each one must not see something

Admins must never see supplier costs. Sellers must never see customers' names, phones or addresses. Managers must never see sellers' private details.

HowHiding it in the UI is not enough, so the rules live in the database. About 1,000 lines of Firestore security rules split private data into its own collections (costs, supplier links, payout details). Sellers see their orders only through a server endpoint that returns order number, date, status and their markup, nothing else. Plan fields such as tier and paid-until can be written only by the server.

02

Prices that cannot be tampered with

A customer's browser builds the order, and a seller sets their own prices. Either could send a wrong number.

HowBefore any payment page opens, the server re-checks every line against the store price and the seller's markup cap, and refuses the payment if anything is off. Promo codes are capped at the seller's markup, so a discount can only ever come out of the seller's earnings, never out of Kyvro's.

03

Payments the way Bangladesh pays

Cards are rare; bKash and Nagad are everywhere, and cash on delivery invites fake orders.

HowOnline payment goes through UddoktaPay (bKash and Nagad), with the amount always read from the saved order on the server. Cash on delivery stays, but the delivery charge (৳100 inside Dhaka, ৳150 outside) is paid online first. Bigger online orders get 10–15% off and free delivery above ৳2,000, which pushes buyers toward paying up front.

04

A whole platform on free hosting

Vercel's free plan allows 12 serverless functions. A store, a seller platform, payments, emails and daily jobs need far more endpoints than that.

HowRelated endpoints share one function and pick their job with an ?action= parameter (plan payments, billing, sitemap, shop removal, AI photos…). The site runs on 11 functions and still has one spare.

05

Product photos that look like a real shop

Supplier photos come in every size and background. Hung on a rail, they looked messy.

HowAn Admin button sends a photo to an image model that puts the garment on a flat colour, picked far from the garment's own colour. The browser then removes that colour to get a clean cut-out, and a person approves it before it goes live. It costs about $0.04 a photo, with a daily limit so a mistake cannot run up the bill.

06

A store that looks after itself

With a one-person team, routine work has to run on its own.

HowA daily job reminds customers about unpaid orders on day 1 and 3 and cancels them on day 7, nudges abandoned carts, sends plan renewal reminders, warns inactive sellers and cleans up removed shops after 30 days.

// Business model

How Kyvro earns

Kyvro earns the margin between supplier cost and store price on every sale. Sellers earn their markup on delivered orders, and pay for plans that unlock more shops, products and branding.
