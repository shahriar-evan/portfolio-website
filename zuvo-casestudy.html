// The challenge

Face-to-face, built for South Asia first

I wanted a social video app where people can go live, meet new friends and talk face to face, built for South Asian users first but usable anywhere. Most apps in this space are mobile-only, English-only, and feel the same.

Real conversations, not a mock-up

Actual video calls that ring the other person, not a demo screen.

One product, two experiences

A full website on laptops and an app-style experience on phones.

Local by default, global by choice

English first, with Bangla and Hindi one tap away.

Safe from day one

Only the right people can join a call, and nobody can tamper with coins.

// Design decisions

Why it looks and works this way

Brand

An original brand

Zuvo Live is short and easy to say in any language, with the tagline “Go live · Meet real · Be you”. The logo is a chat bubble with a play button and a live dot, drawn as SVG so it stays sharp from favicon to splash screen.

Look

Dark neon

A deep purple background with a pink-to-violet gradient and soft glows, so faces and video stand out. Generic “AI sparkle” icons were replaced with meaningful ones: music, mic, coffee, handshake.

Layout

Right layout for each screen

  • On phones it is an app: bottom navigation, a floating Go Live button, full-screen calls
  • On laptops it is a website: a hero with “Start Video Chat”, a live wall of online people, features, pricing and FAQs
Language

A first-class feature

Every screen reads from one translation file. English is the default; after first login users pick their language, and can switch any time from the globe button or their profile.

// How a call works

One call in five steps

A call is a row in the database that both phones watch: the caller creates it, the other person's screen rings, and only after Accept does the server hand each of them a key to the same private video room.

  1. Caller taps Video CallA call record is created with the status “ringing”.
  2. The other phone ringsSupabase Realtime pushes the record to the callee, who sees the incoming call with a ringtone.
  3. Accept or declineThe status moves to accepted, declined, or missed after 30 seconds with no answer.
  4. Server hands out keysOnly a logged-in caller or callee of an accepted call gets a LiveKit video token.
  5. Both join one private roomLiveKit's WebRTC server connects the two cameras; nobody else can join.

Real-timeSupabase Realtime + Presence. Instant call ringing, accept/decline, chat, online status.
HostingVercel. HTTPS (needed for cameras on phones) and auto-deploy on every push.
CodeGit version control, with no secrets in the code.
// Challenges I solved

Problems and fixes

01

A call has to ring the other phone, not just share a room link

Built call signaling: a call record moves ringing → accepted / declined / missed, pushed live to both sides. 30 seconds with no answer ends as “No answer”.

02

Anyone with a room name could join a video call

The server issues a video token only to a logged-in user who is the caller or callee of an accepted call.

03

Users could edit their own coin balance from the browser

Database rules block any self-edit of coins; only server-side logic will change them.

04

The page crashed when the browser blocked the camera

The call now keeps running and shows clear steps to allow camera and mic.

05

Cameras don't work on phones without HTTPS

Deployed on Vercel so the live site runs on HTTPS.

06

Hosting storage was almost full (9.6 of 10 GB)

Cleaned up 514 old deployments of another project, keeping its live site online the whole time.

07

Keys must never leak into the code

Keys live only in local env files and Vercel settings, and the code was checked before and after every push.
